Transient Heat Conduction in a Metal Rod: Time-Stepping and Stability

A Galerkin finite element model of a metal rod cooling toward a steady state, used to compare explicit and implicit time-stepping. The explicit scheme blows up, the implicit one does not.

  • Galerkin FEM
  • θ-method time stepping
  • Quadratic elements
  • Stability analysis
  • Transient heat transfer

The problem

A metal rod of length L = 0.2 m starts at 100 °C. Its left end is insulated, its right end is held at 50 °C, and a heat source acts inside it. Temperature T(x, t) obeys:

∂T/∂t = a ∂²T/∂x² + q,  ∂T/∂x(0) = 0,  T(L) = 50 °C,  T(x, 0) = 100 °C

Given values: k = 2 W/m·K, ρ = 6000 kg/m³, cp = 250 J/kg·K, and a heat source of 1 kJ/s·m³. The assignment asked for six things: derive the weak form, write a finite element code with 3-point Gauss integration that reads its inputs from a file, study mesh and time-step refinement for two schemes, find the time to reach steady state, compute the heat loss at the right end, and replace the insulated end with a fixed heat flux.

Approach

  • Space. Galerkin finite elements turn the equation into a system of ordinary differential equations, [M]{Ṫ} + [K]{T} = {F}. The report used 3-node quadratic elements.
  • Time. A θ-method blends the old and new time levels: θ = 0 is explicit, θ = 0.5 is the trapezoidal rule, θ = 1 is fully implicit.
  • Integration and code. Element integrals use 3-point Gauss quadrature. The code reads the material values, θ, element count, simulated time and time step from a data file.
  • Boundary conditions. The insulated end comes in naturally through the weak form. The fixed 50 °C end is imposed directly.
  • Mesh study. Start with 10 elements and Δt = 100 s, then double the elements and halve Δt, up to 80 elements and 12.5 s.
  • Steady state. The analytical steady-state solution is computed first. The time to steady state is the first time the largest deviation from it falls to about 1%.
  • Further studies. Time to steady state against rod length and source strength, heat loss at the right end over time, and a heat-flux condition on the left end in place of insulation.

What I found

  • The explicit scheme is only conditionally stable. With a time step of 100 s it diverges within the first 10,000 s, with values of order 10¹³. The fully implicit scheme is stable for any time step, and here it matches the analytical solution to within 0.005 °C at every output time.
  • Heat loss at the right end falls exponentially with time and levels off at about 200 W/m².
  • Time to steady state is about 1.2×10⁵ s and grows roughly with the square of rod length, as diffusion scaling predicts. One run shows an unexpected dip near L = 0.5 m that I did not explain.
  • Source strength has only a weak, roughly linear effect: about a 4% drop in settling time across the range tested. A likely reason is the 1% criterion itself. A stronger source raises the steady temperature, so 1% of it is a larger tolerance and is reached sooner.
  • The analytical steady state is T = 50 + (S/2k)(L² − x²). It gives 60 °C on the insulated end and a loss of S·L = 200 W/m² at the right end, and the computed heat-loss curve settles exactly at 200 W/m².
  • Adding a 50 W/m² heat flux on the left end raises the steady temperature, especially on that end, and shortens the time to steady state.
