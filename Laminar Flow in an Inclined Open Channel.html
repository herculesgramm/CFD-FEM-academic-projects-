Laminar Flow in an Inclined Open Channel

A 2D finite element model of viscous flow down a sloped channel with a free surface. It predicts the velocity field, flow rate, Reynolds number and bed shear stress as the slope changes.

  • Galerkin FEM
  • Poisson equation
  • 9-node quadratic elements
  • Banded matrix assembly
  • Open-channel hydraulics
University of Thessaly, Mechanical Engineering. Computational Fluid Dynamics with Finite Elements course, Assignment 2

The problem

A viscous fluid flows down a straight channel of width 0.4 m and depth 0.2 m, tilted at angle θ. Gravity drives the flow along the channel. The fluid sticks to the bed and side walls, and the top is a free surface with no shear. The downstream velocity uz satisfies a Poisson equation:

μ ∇²uz = −ρ g sin θ, with uz = 0 on the bed and walls, ∂uz/∂y = 0 at the surface.

Base case: ρ = 450 kg/m³, μ = 0.8 Pa·s, θ = 10°.

The assignment asked for five things: derive the finite element weak form with 2D quadrilateral elements, answer questions about the connectivity and matrix structure on a 3 × 3 mesh, write a solver with 3-point Gauss integration in each direction, compute mass flow and Reynolds number for slopes from 5° to 30°, and compute the shear stress on the channel bed.

Approach

  • Weak form. Multiplying by a test function and applying Green's theorem gives [K]{u} = {F} + {Q}, with Q holding any boundary flux. On the bed and walls the velocity is fixed, so Q = 0 there. At the free surface the zero-shear condition is natural.
  • Elements. Rectangular 9-node elements with biquadratic Lagrange basis functions. Element integrals use 3-point Gauss quadrature in each direction. A connectivity table places each element's local matrix into the global matrix.
  • Matrix structure. On a 3 × 3 mesh there are 49 nodes, numbered along y. The 5th element's local matrix goes to the rows and columns listed in its connectivity row, so its entry K₁₂ lands at row 17, column 31 of the global matrix. The equation for node 31 gets contributions from elements 4, 5, 7 and 8, and its non-zero columns are 15–19, 22–26, 29–33, 36–40 and 43–47 (25 in total). The bandwidth is 4 × 7 + 5 = 33.
  • Post-processing. Hydraulic diameter Dh = 4A/P = 0.4 m, using the wetted perimeter (bed plus two walls). Reynolds number and mass flow follow from the velocity field. Bed shear stress is μ ∂uz/∂y along the bed.
  • Mesh study. Contour maps at 10×5, 20×10, 40×20 and 80×40.

What I found

  • The velocity peaks at the middle of the free surface and changes sharply near the top corners. The method is stable, and a 20 × 10 mesh already gives a satisfactory picture.
  • Mass flow and Reynolds number grow almost linearly with slope.
  • Flow in channels turns turbulent somewhere between Reynolds numbers of about 750 and 3000. Taking 1875 as the threshold puts the transition near 15°, beyond which the laminar model no longer applies.
  • Bed shear stress has a parabolic profile across the channel, largest at mid-width, and grows with slope.
