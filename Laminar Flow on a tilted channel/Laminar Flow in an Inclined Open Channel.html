Laminar Flow in an Inclined Open Channel

A 2D finite element model of viscous flow down a sloped channel with a free surface. It predicts the velocity field, flow rate, Reynolds number and bed shear stress as the slope changes.

  • Galerkin FEM
  • Poisson equation
  • 9-node quadratic elements
  • 3-point Gauss integration
  • Open-channel hydraulics

The problem

A viscous fluid of density ρ and viscosity μ flows in fully developed laminar flow down a channel with a rectangular cross-section H1 × H2, driven by gravity. The z axis points along the flow and θ is the slope of the channel. The velocity uz(x, y) satisfies a Poisson equation:

μ (∂²uz/∂x² + ∂²uz/∂y²) = −ρ g sin θ

The fluid sticks to the bed and side walls, so uz = 0 there. At the top surface (y = H2) the liquid meets air and the shear is zero, so ∂uz/∂y = 0. Values used: H1 = 40 cm, H2 = 20 cm, θ = 10°, ρ = 450 kg/m³, μ = 0.8 Pa·s.

The assignment asked for: the weak form with bi-quadratic Lagrange quadrilateral elements, a finite element code with 3-point Gauss integration in each direction, velocity plots for four meshes, mass flow and Reynolds number as the slope changes from 5° to 30°, and the bed shear stress for different slopes.

Approach

  • Weak form. Multiplying by a test function and applying Green's theorem gives [K]{u} = {F} + {Q}, with Q holding any boundary flux. On the bed and walls the velocity is fixed, so Q = 0 there. At the free surface the zero-shear condition is natural.
  • Elements. Rectangular 9-node elements with bi-quadratic Lagrange shape functions. The element matrix is computed once per element and added into the global matrix at the element's node positions.
  • Integration and code. Element integrals use 3-point Gauss quadrature in each direction. The code solves the equation with the boundary conditions above for any mesh and slope.
  • Post-processing. Hydraulic diameter Dh = 4A/P = 0.4 m, using the wetted perimeter (bed plus two walls). Reynolds number and mass flow follow from the velocity field. Bed shear stress is μ ∂uz/∂y along the bed.
  • Studies. Velocity contours for 10×5, 20×10, 40×20 and 80×40 meshes at 10°, then a sweep of slope from 5° to 30° on the 40×20 mesh.

What I found

  • The velocity peaks at the middle of the free surface and changes sharply near the top corners. The method is stable, and a 20 × 10 mesh already gives a satisfactory picture.
  • Mass flow and Reynolds number grow almost linearly with slope.
  • Flow in channels turns turbulent somewhere between Reynolds numbers of about 750 and 3000. Taking 1875 as the threshold puts the transition near 15°, beyond which the laminar model no longer applies.
  • Bed shear stress has a parabolic profile across the channel, largest at mid-width, and grows with slope.
